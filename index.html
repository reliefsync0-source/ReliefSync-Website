THE PROBLEM

When networks fail, the shared picture fails

PAGASA reports that about 20 tropical cyclones enter the Philippine Area of Responsibility each year, and 8 or 9 cross the country. Families need food, water, medicine, shelter, transport, and manpower within hours, not days.

Needs arrive by calls, texts, and chat

Requests are scattered and duplicated, with no single list.

HOW IT WORKS

From a cut-off barangay to one verified request list

A store-carry-forward workflow run by your own DRRMO and barangay responders after training. ReliefSync staff do not enter disaster areas to operate it.

1

Record

Trained barangay DRRM volunteers and center managers

Save evacuee counts, requests, inventory, and field reports on phones in offline mode.

2

Carry

Responders assigned by the barangay DRRM committee

Bring phones to the LGU mobile command center or the nearest connected point.

3

Upload

DRRMO staff at the command center

Connect through the satellite link or restored network and run synchronization.

4

Validate

Authorized DRRMO personnel

Review duplicates and conflicts the system flags, then confirm or correct them.

5

Coordinate

Coordinators, NGOs, volunteer groups

View verified requests, accept tasks, and assign volunteers.

6

Close the loop

Field recorders

Confirm deliveries at the next sync. Requests become Delivered with an activity record.

SEE IT IN A TYPHOON

One request, from a cut-off center to Delivered

A typhoon cuts cellular service across a municipality of 25 barangays. This is how a single request moves through ReliefSync.

01

Center A reports 150 evacuees

A trained center manager records a request for food packs and drinking water, offline.

02

A responder carries the phone

The barangay responder brings it to the municipal hall, where the DRRMO runs a satellite-connected command center.

03

The DRRMO syncs and validates

The system flags that a barangay official sent the same request. The validator merges the two.

04

An NGO accepts the task

An NGO with internet access sees one verified request, accepts it, and assigns a delivery team.

05

Delivered, with a record

The center manager confirms receipt. The status moves to Delivered with an activity record.

Without ReliefSync, the same information would travel by radio, text relays, and paper lists and be consolidated by hand.

BUILT FOR OUTAGES

Works on every connection you have, including none

Satellite is an alternative connection, not a guarantee. It needs equipment, an active service, power, and a clear view of the sky, so offline recording always works without it.

Regular internet

WHEN IT APPLIES

Mobile data, Wi-Fi, or wired internet is available.

HOW IT WORKS

Users access the platform and share updates through the central system in real time.

LIMITS

Depends on local networks and power.

Satellite-assisted

WHEN IT APPLIES

Local networks are down, but a command center has satellite equipment.

HOW IT WORKS

Compatible satellite internet connects the center's local network. Responders use ReliefSync through it without relying on nearby cell towers.

LIMITS

Needs equipment, an active service, power, and a clear view of the sky.

Offline

WHEN IT APPLIES

No connection of any kind.

HOW IT WORKS

Supported devices save essential records locally and queue them for synchronization.

LIMITS

Other organizations see records only after sync succeeds. Offline data may be outdated.

ABOUT US

A project team building offline-first disaster coordination

ReliefSync is a project of Mathew Cretecio, Jerald June Asis, and Paul Adrian Paculanang of Davao del Norte State College, built to help institutions coordinate needs, resources, and deliveries when communication networks are disrupted.

Our background

Disaster response often involves many offices, barangays, evacuation centers, NGOs, and private contributors, each keeping separate spreadsheets, paper forms, and messages. When networks and power fail, updated information may never reach the municipal or city DRRMO, and decision-makers work with an incomplete picture.

ReliefSync gives these institutions a shared coordination platform that works online and offline, so needs, offers, allocations, and deliveries stay organized and traceable. It does not replace the authority of LGUs or DRRMOs. It supports their coordination with structured digital records.

OUR VISION

To provide an accessible and reliable disaster-response coordination platform that helps institutions connect community needs with available resources, even when communication networks are disrupted.

OUR MISSION

To improve disaster-response coordination through organized information management, offline-capable field reporting, controlled resource sharing, and accountable delivery tracking.

FAQ

Questions we expect to hear

Does ReliefSync replace our DRRMO or official systems?

No. ReliefSync supports coordination and documentation. LGUs, DRRMOs, and other legally responsible institutions keep authority over official validation and allocation decisions.

Does it work without an internet connection?

Yes for recording. Authorized users save essential records on their phones, and the records upload when a connection is available. Other organizations see them only after synchronization succeeds.

Is Starlink included?

The City / Municipality and Provincial plans include one designated command-center satellite kit and 12 months of service, subject to supplier confirmation. It is not one kit per barangay, and the Barangay and Private / NGO plans do not include it.

Do sponsors control the beneficiary’s workspace?

No. A sponsor pays for an existing plan on behalf of a beneficiary and receives only the funding, utilization, and impact reports agreed in the contract. The beneficiary keeps control of its records and decisions.

How is evacuee data protected?

ReliefSync is designed around data minimization, role-based access, and audit trails, following the Data Privacy Act of 2012. Aggregate counts are preferred over identifiable evacuee information.
